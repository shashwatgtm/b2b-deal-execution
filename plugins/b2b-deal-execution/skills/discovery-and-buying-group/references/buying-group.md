# The buying group: seats, placement, champion test and multi threading

Rewritten for this skill from the method of the Helix ICP intelligence connector (buyer_group_analyzer), read on 14 October 2026. No figure here is a benchmark.

## The five seats
| Seat | Who it is | Usual concern | Next move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | can say yes when others say no, and can find budget | priority, cost of inaction, risk | a short meeting on the business case in their measure |
| Champion | wants the change and sells it inside | their own credibility, the effort of the change | arm them (negotiation-and-champion in this plugin writes the internal asset) |
| Technical evaluator | judges fit with systems, data and security | integration, data handling, workload on their team | a technical session with a written pass mark |
| User | does the work every day | adoption, extra steps, training | a session on their own case; their words in the business case |
| Blocker or reviewer | can stop or slow it (procurement, legal, compliance, audit, security outside a security sale) | risk, policy, precedent | engage early with the documents they need |

## Placing a person in a seat
- Place only the people the user named, by the title the user gave. Never add a person.
- Usual placements (confirm with the user when the deal says otherwise): CFO, CEO, COO, CIO, managing director, business unit head, founder: economic buyer; procurement, legal, compliance, audit, risk, vendor management: blocker or reviewer; IT, CTO, engineers, architects, platform, infrastructure, network, data: technical evaluator; in a cybersecurity sale the CISO is usually the economic buyer and security is not a blocker; the people who do the work: users.
- Finance leaders (head of finance, finance controller): economic buyer or champion; show both readings and ask "Who signs off spend at this level?" Plant, site or regional managers and operations heads: user, or the champion for their site, or the economic buyer for a site budget; show the readings and ask "Is the budget for this held at your site or centrally?" CTO and CIO: technical evaluator or economic buyer; show both unless the buyer's sector note says who signs.
- Champion labels: champion when the user gives evidence for at least two of the three tests below; potential champion when one, or when the user gives only the label (write "test: not shown"); contact when none.
- When a title fits two seats (for example "Head of Platform", "Head of Operations"), show both and the question that settles it ("Who would approve the budget for this, you or someone above you?"). Never decide from one word.
- One person can hold two seats (a founder who is economic buyer and champion); say so.

## The champion test
A champion (1) sells for you when you are not in the room, (2) shares information you would not otherwise get, (3) gives access to the economic buyer and the other seats. One of three: a potential champion. None: a contact. When the user calls someone a champion and also says they block, show both readings and ask the three test questions.

## Who is missing
List every empty seat, with the question to the champion that finds who holds it ("Who in finance will review this?"), and the risk of leaving it empty. Use references/verticals.md for the seats the buyer's sector usually has (for example internal audit in fintech, the TMS owner in logistics tech).

## Multi threading checklist (counts are example defaults)
Each item is met, not met or not shown by the user's facts; the user's own hedge stays.
- Before a proposal: at least three of the five seats engaged (example default; the user sets their own).
- The economic buyer has met the seller or seen the case.
- The technical evaluator has a written pass mark.
- The blockers know the timeline and have the documents they need.
- No seat relies on a single message from the champion.

## The map
A table: person (role as the user gave it), seat, why that seat, their concern, what they need to see, the next move with a date if the user has one, and status (only as the user gave it, otherwise not known). Then "Missing seats" and "Questions to place the uncertain ones". An influence line (who approves, who influences, who reviews) may follow in plain sentences; no diagram is required.

## Privacy
Seats and professional concerns only. No personality profile, no private details, no gender for a person known only by a role. Research on real named people is for b2b-research-market-and-account / account-research.
