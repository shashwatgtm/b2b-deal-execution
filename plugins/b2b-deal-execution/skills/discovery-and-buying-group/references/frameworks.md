# Discovery frameworks: what each part must find out

Rewritten for this skill from the method of the Helix revenue enablement connector (discovery_question_bank), read on 14 October 2026. The question shells are examples; the subject of every question is the user's own pain, product part or buyer, in the buyer's words.

## The habits behind every framework
- One question per pain the user named: never fold three pains into one question.
- Ask about the buyer's own measure ("how do you measure ... today, and who owns that number?"), never offer a number.
- Ask the signer as the signer (budget, priority, risk), the user as the user (daily work), the technical evaluator as the technical evaluator (systems, data, security).
- Mark what is already known from the user's notes, and ask only the open parts.
- Open with the reason for the call in the buyer's terms; close with a playback (what we heard), the open points, and a dated next step.

## MEDDPICC
| Part | What it must find out | Example shell |
|---|---|---|
| Metrics | the buyer's own measure of the problem and of success | "How do you measure [pain] today, and what would a good result look like to you?" |
| Economic buyer | who can say yes and find budget | "Who besides you needs to agree before a change like this goes ahead?" |
| Decision criteria | what they will judge options on | "What will you compare options on, and which of those matters most?" |
| Decision process | the steps and dates to a decision | "Walk me through how a decision like this was made the last time." |
| Paper process | legal, security, procurement, vendor registration | "Once you decide, what happens before a contract can be signed, and who runs each step?" |
| Identify pain | the pain, its cause and its cost | "What happens today when [pain] occurs, and what does it cost the team?" |
| Champion | who sells for us inside | "Who on your side would explain this to the team if we were not there?" |
| Competition | other options, including doing nothing or building it | "What else are you looking at, including keeping things as they are?" (neutral; landmine questions against a named competitor are for b2b-sales-enablement / competitive-intelligence) |

## BANT
| Part | What it must find out | Example shell |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | whether money is set aside or must be found, and who owns it | "Is there money set aside for fixing [pain] this year, or would it need to be found?" |
| Authority | who signs and who can stop it | "Who would sign off a change like this, and who could stop it?" |
| Need | the pain and its cost in the buyer's measure | "What does [pain] cost the team today, in the way you measure it?" |
| Timeline | the event or date that sets the timing | "Is there a date or event by which this needs to be solved?" |

## SPICED
| Part | What it must find out | Example shell |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | how things work today (systems, team, volume in their words) | "Walk me through how [process] works today." |
| Pain | what goes wrong, one pain at a time | "Where does [process] break down?" |
| Impact | what the pain costs, in the buyer's measure (time, money, risk, customers) | "When [pain] happens, what does it cost you?" |
| Critical event | the date or event that makes waiting costly | "What happens if nothing changes by [the date they named]?" |
| Decision | who decides, how and on what criteria | "How will the decision be made, and on what?" |

## Challenger (teach, tailor, take control)
- Teach: a question that offers a view the buyer may not have considered, grounded in the user's own proof or the buyer's sector notes, never in an invented statistic ("Teams that re plan routes only once a day often find the failed deliveries cluster after noon; is that what you see?" is allowed only if the user gave that observation; otherwise ask it open: "When in the day do failed deliveries cluster for you?").
- Tailor: the same point in the words of each seat (the finance view, the operations view, the IT view).
- Take control: a question that fixes the next step and who attends.

## Gap Selling
| Part | What it must find out | Example shell |
|---|---|---|
| Current state | the facts of today, the problems and their impact | "What does [process] look like today, and what does it cost?" |
| Future state | what they want instead, in their measure | "If this worked the way you want, what would be different in your numbers?" |
| The gap | the distance between the two, and what closing it is worth to them | "What is closing that gap worth to you this year?" |
| Problems behind the problem | the root cause, so the answer fits it | "Why does [pain] happen in the first place?" |

## When the user names no framework
Ask once which framework the team uses. If none or "any", use a plain set: situation, pain (one per pain), impact in their measure, who decides and how, timing, and the next step; say this is a default the user can swap for MEDDPICC, BANT, SPICED, Challenger or Gap Selling.

## Depth by stage
- First call: situation and pain, few questions, more listening; no budget question early in the call (the user sets how early).
- Discovery: impact, the measure, the people involved, the process.
- Deep dive or technical: systems, data, integration, security, acceptance tests, who signs off each.
- Executive: priorities, the cost of inaction, the decision and its timing, risk.
