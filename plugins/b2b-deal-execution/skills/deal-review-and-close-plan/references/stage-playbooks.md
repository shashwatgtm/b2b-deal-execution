# Stage playbooks for one B2B deal

Rewritten for this skill from the stage strategies of the Helix revenue enablement connector (deal_strategy_coach), read on 14 October 2026. No figure here is a benchmark; where a count appears it is an example default the user can change.

Each stage has: the objective, the evidence that the stage is really done (exit evidence), the questions to answer, and the usual risk. A deal is in the stage whose exit evidence is NOT yet complete, whatever the CRM says; say so when the user's stage and the evidence disagree.

## Prospecting
- Objective: earn the first meeting with the right person.
- Exit evidence: a meeting held with a person who feels the problem; an agenda the buyer agreed; a second person aware.
- Questions: why would they meet now (a trigger they would recognise)? Which problem can we help with, in their words? Who is the right entry point?
- Usual risk: a meeting with someone who cannot act.

## Discovery
- Objective: understand the problem and what it costs the buyer, in the buyer's own measures.
- Exit evidence: the problem stated in business terms with the buyer's own measure; more than one stakeholder engaged; the buying process and the signer known; next steps agreed with dates.
- Questions: what does inaction cost them? Who else is involved? What is their timeline and process? What would success look like to them?
- Usual risk: a single thread, and a pain that nobody has put a measure on.

## Demo (or walkthrough for a services, connectivity or hardware seller)
- Objective: connect what we sell to the buyer's own needs, on their case.
- Exit evidence: the people who matter agree it fits; the technical validation path is clear; the business case conversation is booked; objections are out in the open.
- Questions: what would make this session a success for them? Who else should see it? What worries them most about a change like this?
- Usual risk: a feature tour that answers no stated pain.

## Proposal
- Objective: an offer the economic buyer can say yes to.
- Exit evidence: the proposal reviewed with the champion first; the economic buyer has seen the business case; a date for the decision; procurement engaged.
- What the proposal carries (the document itself is written with b2b-sales-enablement / sales-proposal-builder): summary, the problem in the buyer's words, the approach, the business case on the buyer's figures, the investment, the proof, the next steps.
- Usual risk: the proposal goes straight to procurement and is read as a price.

## Negotiation
- Objective: close while protecting value.
- Rules: never give a concession without getting something back; trade terms (payment, length, timing, scope, a reference), do not cut the unit price first; keep the executive sponsor engaged. The price arithmetic and the trade list live in negotiation-and-champion in this plugin.
- Exit evidence: terms agreed that the seller can deliver on; a reference or case study commitment where the user wants one.
- Usual risk: a late discount with nothing in return.

## Closing
- Objective: signature and the start of delivery.
- Exit evidence (a checklist): technical validations complete; legal redlines resolved; security and compliance approved; budget confirmed; procurement complete; signature authority confirmed; the kick off date set.
- Usual risk: a step on the buyer's side that nobody owns.

## Stuck deal
- Objective: re qualify or close it out.
- Kill criteria to put to the user (they decide): the champion has left or disengaged; the budget moved; a competitor was selected; no reply after several attempts (the user sets how many; 3 is an example default); the problem is not urgent for them.
- Action: one direct message to the champion or signer that names the stall and asks a yes or no question; a deadline the user sets (two weeks is an example default) to either restart with a dated next step or move it out of the pipeline.

## The champion test
A champion is someone who (1) sells for you when you are not in the room, (2) shares information you would not otherwise get (the process, the competition, the budget), and (3) gives you access to the economic buyer and the other seats. Someone who does one of the three is a potential champion; none is a contact.

## The economic buyer
The person who can say yes when everyone else says no, and can find budget that was not planned. "Named" means the user knows who it is; "engaged" means the person has met or replied to the seller about this deal. A buyer the user calls disengaged, no longer engaged or not engaged is named, not engaged.

## Five coaching questions for any deal review
1. Why would they buy at all (the problem and its cost, in their words)?
2. Why us (what the buyer said, not what we say)?
3. Why now (the event or date that makes waiting costly)?
4. Who decides, and how (the signer and the steps)?
5. What can go wrong (the risks, and what we do about each)?
