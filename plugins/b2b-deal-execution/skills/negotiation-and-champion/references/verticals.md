# Sector notes for deals in nine B2B verticals

Read the section for the BUYER's sector (the company the seller is selling to), not the seller's own sector, unless they are the same. These notes are vocabulary, the usual buying committee, usual objections with the pattern of a good answer, the usual sales motion, the metrics the sector watches (names only) and the shape of a good proof point. They hold no statistic, market size, benchmark or company fact: a number in a deal comes from the user or from a page opened and cited in this session.

Match the buyer to the nearest section by what the buyer does, not by a heading word: a bank, NBFC or other lender reads Fintech for its compliance, audit, risk and IT review seats only; a consumer goods maker that sells through distributors reads the consumer goods distribution lines of Vertical SaaS; a quick commerce or delivery company reads Logistics tech for its operations seats only. A telecom operator buying software for its own IT or platform teams reads the operator platform line of Telecom, not the seller's Software or SaaS section. A retailer or any other buyer with no near section uses the seats the seller gave. Name the section you used once, and use only the seats named here, as hedged prompts ("usually"). When no section is near, use the seats the seller gave and say so in one line. When the buyer's sector is unclear from the user's words, ask once. Never place a buyer in a sector because one word matched (a "delivery" or "security" in a sentence does not make a company logistics or cybersecurity). A services firm is not sold like a software subscription: no free trial, seats or "aha moment" for ITeS, telecom connectivity or hardware buyers unless the user wrote them.

## Logistics tech
- Words: dispatch, fleet, last mile, route plan, first attempt delivery, proof of delivery, 3PL, cost per delivery, delivery SLA, TMS.
- Committee: the COO or Head of Supply Chain signs; the Head of Logistics or Last Mile Operations champions; fleet and dispatch managers use it daily; IT checks the fit with the TMS, WMS and order systems; finance checks the cost per delivery case.
- Objections and the pattern of a good answer: "We already have a TMS" (ask which daily decisions the TMS does not make, position as working with it); "Drivers will not use a new app" (a pilot at one hub, adoption measure agreed first); "Integration effort" (name the systems and the few fields, and who owns each on the buyer side); "Margins are too thin" (tie the price to cost per delivery and failed deliveries in the buyer's own data).
- Motion: enterprise sale with a pilot at one hub or city, often timed around peak season planning.
- Metrics watched: cost per delivery, first attempt delivery rate, on time delivery, deliveries per vehicle per day, vehicle utilisation, failed delivery and return rate, dispatch planning time.
- Proof shape: a before and after at one hub, measured over a full cycle of busy and quiet weeks.

## Fintech
- Words: reconciliation, month end close, policy controls, audit trail, accounts payable, ERP posting, compliance review, approval workflow, data residency.
- Committee: the CFO signs; the Finance Controller or the head of the affected finance team champions; internal audit and compliance review controls; IT checks ERP integration and security.
- Objections: "Our ERP already does this" (show the gap on one month of the buyer's own transactions); "Security and compliance review" (prepare data residency, access, audit log and certification answers you actually hold); "Migration will disrupt the close" (cut over just after a month end, run in parallel for one cycle); "Regulatory caution" (map each named requirement to the control that meets it; never claim a status you cannot show).
- Motion: CFO led with a security and compliance review inside the cycle; proof through a pilot on one entity or department.
- Metrics watched: days to close the books, reconciliation effort, policy breach rate, approval cycle time, audit findings, cost per transaction, payment success rate.
- Proof shape: close time or reconciliation effort before and after for one entity, signed off by the finance controller.

## SaaS (horizontal software subscriptions)
- Words: activation, time to value, net revenue retention, renewal, expansion, onboarding, usage, churn, customer success.
- Committee: the budget owner of the function signs; the team lead who feels the problem champions; end users adopt it; finance and IT review cost, security and integrations.
- Objections: "We built this in house" (compare the cost of maintaining it, including the people); "The price grows as we grow" (align the pricing metric with the value, show the price at the next stage); "Switching cost" (migration plan and time to first value); "Another tool to log into" (show where it sits in the tools they use).
- Motion: product led or sales assisted; trials where buyers expect them; expansion through usage and renewals.
- Metrics watched: activation rate, time to value, net revenue retention, logo churn, expansion revenue, payback on acquisition cost, usage depth.
- Proof shape: a cohort of customers before and after, on activation, retention or expansion.

## Vertical SaaS (for example field sales, distribution and retail execution software for consumer goods; field service software)
- Words (consumer goods distribution): distributor, outlet, beat plan, secondary sales, general trade, SKU, order capture, retail execution, DMS, trade scheme. For field service software, use the buyer's own words (technician, job, work order, site visit, first time fix) and ask when unsure.
- Committee: the National Sales Head or Managing Director signs; the Head of Sales Operations champions; regional managers and field reps use it; distributors hold stock and order data; IT checks ERP and DMS integration. For field service, the head of service operations usually signs and field managers champion; confirm with the user.
- Objections: "Reps will not use another app" (one region first, offline on low end phones, incentives tied to use); "Our DMS vendor bundles an app" (compare what reps can do in the outlet, not the feature list); "Data is not in sync" (agree how stock and orders flow back before rollout); "A national rollout takes too long" (region by region, a measured pilot region first).
- Motion: enterprise sale led by the sales or service function, a pilot region before a national rollout.
- Metrics watched: secondary sales, productive calls, lines per call, outlet coverage, strike rate, order fill rate, distributor claim settlement time.
- Proof shape: the pilot region against a comparable region without the product.

## AI native
- Words: resolution rate, evaluation set, human in the loop, guardrails, accuracy, hallucination, data privacy, inference cost, automation rate.
- Committee: the owner of the workflow being automated signs; the data or AI lead evaluates the model; security and legal review data use and privacy; the operations team that hands work to the AI uses it. For AI infrastructure (compute, cloud), the CTO or head of platform signs and ML engineers evaluate; confirm with the user.
- Objections: "AI gets answers wrong" (an evaluation on the buyer's own history before go live, a person approves any action that moves money or changes a record); "Data privacy" (where data is processed and stored, what is used for training, how it is deleted); "We can build this on a model API" (compare the full cost of evaluation, guardrails and integrations); "Cost grows with volume" (cost per resolved case against today's cost, on the buyer's volumes).
- Motion: a proof of concept on the buyer's own data, then a production pilot with guardrails and human review.
- Metrics watched: automated resolution rate, accuracy on an evaluation set, escalation rate, handling time, cost per resolution, satisfaction on automated cases.
- Proof shape: results on an evaluation set from the buyer's own history, then live results with human review on.

## ITeS (IT services, managed services, BPM)
- Words: SLA, statement of work, transition, steady state, service credits, governance, ticket backlog, knowledge transfer, managed service.
- Committee: the CIO or business unit head signs; the IT operations or service owner champions; procurement and vendor management run the commercial process; finance checks rates; security checks access and compliance.
- Objections: "Transition risk from the incumbent" (a staged transition with knowledge transfer, a parallel run and exit criteria per stage); "Your rates are higher" (compare the total cost of the outcome, not the hourly rate); "Attrition and key people" (team model, backups, documentation); "Lock in" (clear exit terms, documentation the client owns).
- Motion: RFP led or relationship led; proposals carry a transition plan, a governance model and references from similar clients. No free trial or seats.
- Metrics watched: SLA attainment, mean time to resolve, first contact resolution, backlog age, cost per ticket or per FTE, satisfaction, transition milestones met.
- Proof shape: SLA and cost outcomes at a similar client, with the transition timeline actually met.

## Telecom (connectivity, network equipment, operators)
- Words: SD-WAN, MPLS, internet leased line, uptime, SLA, latency, branch sites, last mile link, network operations centre, site survey. For network equipment makers (radio access, core), use the buyer's own words (field trial, interoperability, certification, rollout) and ask when unsure.
- Committee: the CIO signs (for an operator buying equipment, the CTO or network head); the network or infrastructure head champions; the CISO reviews security; procurement compares rate cards or tenders; finance checks the cost per site.
- Objections: "Price per site is higher" (total cost per site, including outages and repair time); "Migration risk across many sites" (a wave plan with fallback links and a rollback rule); "Long relationship with our current operator" (start where service is worst); "Security overlay" (who manages network and security together and who responds to an incident).
- Motion: account based enterprise sale, often an RFP or tender, with a site survey or field trial before rollout.
- Metrics watched: uptime per site, mean time to repair, latency, sites live per wave, cost per site, incidents per month, service credits paid.
- Proof shape: uptime and repair time across pilot sites against the incumbent's record for the same sites.
- An operator's own IT or platform team buying software: read this line, not the Software or SaaS section, when the buyer is a telecom operator and the product is software for its IT, platform or engineering teams (for example a developer platform, BSS or OSS tools, a data platform).
  - Words: internal developer platform, environment provisioning, cloud cost, release governance, change advisory board, architecture review, BSS and OSS stack, vendor onboarding, security assessment, build or buy.
  - Seats, as usual patterns to test, not facts about this buyer: the CTO or CIO of the operator usually signs; the head of platform engineering, DevOps or IT usually champions; the IT or network security team usually reviews access and data; an architecture review or change advisory board may need to approve; procurement usually runs vendor onboarding and sometimes a tender; finance may compare the cost with building in house.
  - Questions to ask: Which budget does this come from, IT, network or a platform team? Who decides build or buy, and has an in house option been costed? Does an architecture review or change advisory board need to see it, and when does it meet? What does vendor onboarding or a security assessment involve on your side, and who runs it?

## Software (developer tools, APIs, open source)
- Words: CI pipeline, developer experience, test coverage, release frequency, API, SDK, technical debt, open source alternative, mean time to recovery.
- Committee: the VP Engineering or CTO signs; a team or platform lead champions; developers use it daily; security reviews code and data access; procurement handles seats or usage.
- Objections: "Our developers use open source tools" (compare the time spent maintaining them on one team); "Per user cost at our scale" (tie price to the teams that use it and the time saved in a trial); "Migrating our scripts and tests" (migrate one real project in the trial); "Security review of code access" (state what the product reads and stores, documentation up front).
- Motion: developers adopt first (trial or free tier where offered), then a team or enterprise deal led by engineering leadership.
- Metrics watched: release frequency, lead time for changes, escaped defects, test coverage, build time, mean time to recovery, developer time saved.
- Proof shape: release frequency or escaped defects on one team before and after, from the team's own pipeline data.

## Cybersecurity
- Words: attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect, mean time to respond, SOC, compliance audit, risk register, threat intelligence. For security services (VAPT, audits), use scope, findings, retest and report words.
- Committee: the CISO signs; the SOC or cloud security lead champions; security engineers use it; risk, compliance and audit review the evidence; the CIO or CTO checks integration.
- Objections: "We already have a tool" (map the overlap honestly, show what it misses on the buyer's environment); "Too many alerts" (rank findings by real exposure); "Integration with SIEM and ticketing" (name what exists today, test it in the proof of value); "Proof before budget" (a time boxed proof of value with written success criteria).
- Motion: CISO led, with a proof of value on the buyer's own environment; budget often follows an audit finding or an incident.
- Metrics watched: mean time to detect, mean time to respond, critical exposures open, alerts per analyst, audit findings, time to prepare an audit, asset coverage.
- Proof shape: exposures found and closed during the proof of value, with the time it took to fix them.

## Commercial trades by business model (for a concession, no figures)
- Software subscription: a longer term, payment upfront, more users or a wider rollout, a case study and reference rights.
- Services: a longer contract term, a wider scope, volume commitments, a reference call after go live.
- Connectivity: a longer term, more sites or links, a faster cut over agreed by both sides, a reference visit.
- Per transaction: committed monthly volume, a longer term, exclusivity for a product line or region, a case study.
- Hardware plus software: a larger device order, a longer software term, payment upfront, a reference site.
