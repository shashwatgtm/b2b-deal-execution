# The champion's internal selling assets

Rewritten for this skill from the method of the Helix revenue enablement connector (champion_enablement_kit), read on 14 October 2026. A champion asset is written IN THE CHAMPION'S VOICE, for the champion's own colleagues (their CFO, their leadership, their IT team), not as a seller's document. Client facing proposals, decks and business case documents are b2b-sales-enablement / sales-proposal-builder.

## Rules for every asset
1. The champion's voice: first person plural for their company ("we", "our team"), plain words their colleagues use, no seller language ("best in class", "leading").
2. Built only from the seller's facts and the buyer's own words the seller passed on. A vendor claim with no source (an ROI multiple, a percentage improvement) is not written into the asset: the asset carries a placeholder for the claim and its source, the figure is shown only in the note to the champion as the user's claim with no source, and the asset proposes measuring the result in a pilot instead. No return figure unless measured at this buyer or given with a source.
3. One decision requested, stated plainly, with the date it is needed.
4. The risks are named honestly; what reduces each is only what the seller offered or reported (a staged start, a rollback rule, exit terms), otherwise "[User to add: what reduces this risk]". A good answer pattern in references/verticals.md is a suggestion for the seller to confirm, never a promise in the asset. Colleagues trust a memo that names risks.
5. Short: a busy reader finishes it in two minutes.
6. Placeholders for what the seller does not know: "[User to add: ...]". How the product handles data is a placeholder unless the seller gave it or a cited page shows it (SKILL.md rule 15).
7. A note to the champion on what to check before forwarding, and on their own stake only as far as the seller described it.
8. Fill every slot the seller's message or a script line answers, leave "[User to add: ...]" only where nothing does. Trades the skill recommends stay in the note to the seller, never in the asset as agreed.

## Asset shapes
- Internal email to a colleague (for example to the CFO): subject; the problem in the colleague's terms; what we propose; what it costs (the seller's figure); what we expect and how we will measure it; the risks and how we reduce them; the decision requested and by when.
- Executive brief or memo: To and From lines (roles); recommendation in one sentence; what it is; why now; alternatives considered (including doing nothing); cost; questions and answers; risks; next steps; decision requested.
- Presentation talking points: a short slot (the champion sets the minutes; a 15 minute slot split 2, 3, 4, 4 and 2 minutes is an example default): the problem, the option, the evidence, the risks, the ask.
- Objection answers for the champion: each likely question from a colleague, answered in the champion's voice, with "what could go wrong" and the honest answer.
- Risk register: risk, likelihood in words (the champion's judgement, never a percentage made up), what reduces it, owner.

## The reader
Shape the asset for the reader's seat (references/verticals.md for the buyer's sector). These are general reader notes, not facts about this person: a finance reader usually wants the cost, the measure and the risk; an IT reader usually wants systems, data and security; an operations reader usually wants the daily work and the rollout. In the answer, write the note with "usually" and name it as the plugin's reader notes; what this reader will ask or judge is a question for the champion.
