# Holding the price in a new B2B deal: playbooks

Rewritten for this skill from the method of the Helix revenue enablement connector (pricing_negotiation_guide), read on 14 October 2026. No figure here is a benchmark; every number in an answer is the seller's own, run through scripts/concession_math.py. Renewal negotiations are not here: b2b-customer-success / renewal-save-plan.

## Rules for every scenario
1. Understand the real ask before answering: what is behind the request (budget timing, a competing offer, a procurement target, a habit)? Ask one question first.
2. Reframe value, not price: go back to the buyer's own measure of the problem (from discovery) and what the change is worth to them.
3. Never give a concession without getting something back. Trade terms before unit price: contract length, payment timing, volume or scope, the start date, a case study or reference, a faster decision date.
4. Put every trade in writing as a pair: "If you can do X, we can do Y."
5. Know the walk away point before the meeting; the seller sets it, never the plugin.
6. Keep the economic buyer and the champion engaged and keep the value conversation with them; give procurement what it needs to compare offers, and never tell the seller what procurement wants or is doing beyond what the seller reported.
7. Show the cost of every concession by script (revenue at risk, value after discount, the term total), so the seller sees what is given.

## Words to say and not say (examples of the pattern, adapt to the seller's voice)
- Say: "Help me understand what is driving the number." Not: "What discount do you need?"
- Say: "If we move on price, what can you move on: the term, the start date, the scope?" Not: "Let me see what I can do."
- Say: "That is outside what I can approve alone; here is what I can do today." Not: "OK, fine."

## Discount request
Situation table (value, discount asked, revenue at risk, value after discount, by script); what is behind the ask; the trades to offer in return (the business model list in references/verticals.md, priced by the seller); the approval path (who in the seller's company approves which level; the seller gives it); the words.

## Budget objection
Diagnose first: no budget at all, budget in the wrong year, budget in another department, or a price that is too high for the value seen. Options: phase the scope, move the start date into the next budget year, a smaller first phase with a dated expansion, or find the budget owner with the champion. When the budget is truly not there, say so and agree when to come back.

## Competitor price
Use only the gap the buyer stated (concession_math.py gap). Ask what is inside the cheaper offer (scope, service, terms, total cost over the term, switching cost). Answer on the buyer's own criteria; no claim about the other vendor's product. Battle cards and landmine questions: b2b-sales-enablement / competitive-intelligence.

## Procurement pressure
Separate procurement from the business sponsor; give procurement what it needs to compare (scope, terms, documents) and keep the value conversation with the sponsor. Terms to protect: price floor, payment terms, liability limits the seller's company sets, renewal terms. Terms to trade: length, timing, volume, reference rights.

## Multi year
Compare the terms year by year by script (concession_math.py multiyear): list price, discount per year, totals, the amount given up. Ask for something per year of commitment (payment upfront, a volume floor, a reference).

## Enterprise agreement
A table of the units involved, who signs for each, what each needs; a phased rollout with a dated plan per unit; terms that hold across units (price protection, a single paper) and terms that differ.

## End of quarter pressure
Show the cost of the concession by script; name the trade to ask for; name the risk of teaching the buyer to wait for the quarter end. The decision is the seller's.
